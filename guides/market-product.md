# Separate the promise from the product

A marketing page helps a visitor understand an offer and decide to try it. A product interface helps them finish a task. They share a brand kit, but their content, hierarchy and success criteria are different.

## The marketing brief

State the audience, specific problem, useful promise and primary conversion. Plan the opening message, product or editorial imagery, feature explanations, an honest demonstration, relevant pricing or offer details, objections answered in an FAQ, and a final path into the product. Give each section a purpose. Avoid fabricated customer counts, reviews, endorsements, scarcity or guarantees.

“Full marketing” within a website means a complete explanation and conversion journey. It does not authorize ads, outreach, social posting, email campaigns or external promotion. A crawler block remains in place when requested; do not remove it to improve promotion.

## Plan the whole scroll from the mockup

Inspect the complete mockup, including the first section below its opening. Map its composition to live sections: image scale, column relationships, portrait or product rails, full-width bands, compact information rows and longer editorial passages. Continue that visual language down the page. Do not implement only the hero and repeat a generic three-card feature section beneath it. Record which decisions come from the mockup and which are proposed continuations.

Use vertical space for a developed story. Let visitors discover the collection, understand why it fits their need, compare choices, see the product in use and take a relevant next action. A longer page needs new information at each stage. Neither large empty padding nor repeated feature slogans constitutes content. Give substantial imagery, readable copy and useful detail room to coexist. Do not force every section to fit a viewport or reduce type to fit more content.

Before implementation, write a section map with: visitor question, concrete content, supporting asset or demonstration, layout pattern and next action. Draft the actual content before assigning heights. Include meaningful product descriptions, differences, use cases, examples, offer details and answers to practical objections when relevant. Do not invent production specifications, social proof or policies; label fictional sample data clearly.

## Choose a format that helps sell this offer

Select layouts from the actual content and mockup rather than applying every pattern everywhere:

- Product comparisons: a legible grid or responsive table with consistent fields and specific differences.
- Fashion, objects or destinations: an editorial lookbook or a carousel with a substantial image, its story and a relevant product link.
- People or collections: a portrait grid with useful filters and enough context to make a choice.
- Events: a programme or schedule with synopsis, session, price and a direct path to selection.
- A planner or service: an itinerary, annotated workflow or realistic example output.
- A creative tool: a varied reference board with notes explaining how the pieces produce a direction.
- A reading or editorial product: excerpts written for the example, annotations, reading paths or a magazine-like composition.

Vary the rhythm across the whole page: wide artwork, multi-column comparison, a focused demonstration, a narrative sequence and a clear offer. Keep DOM reading order logical. Use grids to relate content, not to box every sentence. A collection of sites must have distinct content structures and selling arguments, beyond different colors and fonts.

## Carousels

Use a carousel only when viewing one substantial item at a time serves the material. Follow the interaction contract in [Interface rules](interface-rules.md); keep essential decision content visible outside it.

## Verify content and composition, not just controls

Inspect the entire vertical page, its section transitions and a small-laptop opening, plus 320px reflow and 200% text. Check both carousel ends, direct selection, Show all, filtered and empty states, and product-specific links. Verify that selecting a named item lands on that item. Every section must answer a different useful question, and every CTA must describe the outcome it actually provides. A page is incomplete when it is mostly a hero, recycled features and a final button.

## The product UI brief

Use [Page mockups](page-mockups.md) for both briefs. Inventory every navigation link; generate and inspect structural alternatives while exploring styles; select a direction; then condition each page and substantial continuation on both the actual brand kit and selected direction. Do this before writing layout code. A single marketing hero and one generic app grid do not cover a multi-page site. Keep each product's selling structure distinct across a family of examples.

Define the primary job as an action with an outcome: configure a light and save its specification, build a cart, record a plant-care task, shortlist a performer, or plan a reading session. Describe the data, navigation, controls, initial state, changed states, empty and error states, and the output the user receives.

Do not paste a marketing hero into the app and call it a working product. Do not use a drawing of a button as a control. Render real labels, navigation, forms and outputs. For a local demonstration, state that orders, tickets and bookings are illustrative and no payment or request is sent.

## Create the right content assets

For commerce, generate separate product views with coherent silhouettes, believable materials and enough room for consistent crops. Put exact product names, prices and variants in HTML. For a library or planner, generate covers, destinations or editorial thumbnails that suit the task. Keep fictional people and products labeled in the case study. Never fill a production catalogue with unrelated stock-like images just to occupy space.

Write an asset manifest before generation: placement, role, subject, aspect ratio, original treatment anchor, and exclusions. The brand kit can contain the first inspected product images. Later variants should use the same original and the locked identity decisions; avoid a chain of increasingly distant derivatives.

## Deliver the requested surfaces

When both surfaces are requested, link the marketing page to the actual app or storefront and back. A single storefront may combine promotion with browsing; separate its briefs and task states without inventing an unrequested app. Supply the HTML brand kit, generated logo source and any separately requested vector adaptation, typography sources, tokens, exact image and mockup prompts, raw outputs, palettes, finishing decisions and working source. A process page should show the chronological dependency without mixing a UI prompt into a marketing prompt.

If repository stars are shown, use the real repository count. A static fallback needs its last-checked date; a failed API request must not turn into a fabricated count or zero. Link the repository directly and distinguish the current response from the saved snapshot.
