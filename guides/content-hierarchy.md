# Give the visitor a reason to continue

Use this guide when planning a site, restructuring content, or redesigning navigation and case-study pages. Establish the page's purpose before choosing a layout. An attractive asset library does not explain the product that created it.

## Order content around the reader's decision

Record who is arriving, what they know, the question they need answered and the primary action. Reuse the established purpose and tone. Give each section a job: explain the offer, demonstrate it, answer a practical question, or help the reader act. Remove duplicate explanations and preserve useful detail.

For a site marketing a tool or skill, explain what it does and who it helps before presenting a large catalog or technical archive. Show concrete deliverables and an accessible starting action near the introduction. Follow with a short workflow and relevant evidence. Place the catalog later or on its own route while keeping direct access for returning visitors. A search product, shop or library may need a different order; derive it from the task rather than imposing a universal landing-page formula.

Write the intended reading sequence into the brief. A workflow skill might move from its promise to outputs, process, a composed identity, working examples and deeper references. Explain each transition. Reordering navigation alone does not fix document hierarchy.

Headings should tell the story when read in sequence. Use one page heading, meaningful section headings and subordinate labels. Align DOM order with visual reading order. Put actions where the reader has enough information to use them, and name their outcomes clearly. Scale whitespace and image size to section importance; avoid both empty poster sections and dense inventories without an introduction.

## Make brand-kit pages useful case studies

Distinguish the editable identity board from the page explaining it. The board combines the generated logo, imported typography, coordinated images and labeled palette. Preserve its inspected source and screenshot as evidence. The companion page explains how those choices serve the product.

Introduce the product, audience and tone. Present the complete board once at a useful size, with access to its full-size screenshot and editable HTML. Do not place the full live board and an identical full screenshot one after another.

Explain the font pairing and semantic colors in readable live content. Separate curated UI tokens from measured artwork palettes. Show the logo and images with their roles. Keep long prompts, raw data, alternate font research and correction records in labeled disclosures or dedicated detail views. These must remain reachable by mouse and keyboard. Preserve exact historical records.

Then show how the identity informed the selected mockups and working site. Pair a concrete observation with its preview: a portrait arrangement became a casting roster, or a specimen sheet became a plant shelf. Place a clear example-site action after that explanation. Retain a direct contextual link for returning visitors rather than enforcing a sequential gate.

Shared templates may serve related collections, but each page needs its own purpose, decisions, board, assets and evidence. Preserve each brand's identity; do not use interchangeable explanations across unrelated products.

## Give navigation a clear scope

Distinguish site-wide destinations, local sections and an example product's own navigation. Label the groups and show the active destination. Replace ambiguous repeated labels such as "Explore" with meaningful destination names.

Keep primary navigation short and ordered by the site's purpose. Group secondary material under a labeled disclosure when useful. Keep the main action visible. On narrow screens, use an explicit menu control instead of a long link list consuming the first screen. Provide a usable no-JavaScript fallback.

Within examples, preserve the product's brand and primary task while providing a consistent route to its brand kit, process and parent site. Keep this contextual navigation visually subordinate to the product. Do not make the parent site's marketing links look like transaction controls.

Apply the [navigation menu contract](ui-components.md#navigation-menus) for semantics,
open/close behavior, focus and responsive transitions. The [menu alignment rules](interface-rules.md#navigation-menu-alignment)
govern header tracks, dropdown edges, unequal columns, wrapped labels and controls.
Keep these procedures canonical rather than creating a different contract for each page.

## Generate and verify the structure

For a structural redesign, follow [REDESIGN](../skills/redesign/SKILL.md). Attach the existing page and inspected brand kit to generate the proposed hierarchy before coding. Generate alternatives when comparing structures, including substantial lower-page continuations. Cover menus closed, expanded and at narrow widths. Shared navigation can use component references across existing pages without inventing new page compositions for unchanged content.

Inventory artwork inside the selected mockup. Reuse matching separately generated assets with their actual records, or generate new assets independently. Never crop artwork from the UI concept.

Verify the complete introduction-to-kit-to-example-and-return journey. Exercise every affected destination, local section link, disclosure and primary action. Inspect desktop, 320px reflow and 200% text, including long labels and expanded menus. Confirm technical detail remains accessible without overwhelming the introduction. Record observed results and refresh affected previews and downloads.
